ACADEMIC ENGLISH

Learn the language of study.

Build the vocabulary and communication skills needed for school, university, research and academic writing.

UNDERSTAND THE BASICS

What is academic English?

Academic English is the type of English commonly used in education, research, university lectures, essays, reports and formal discussions.

It is usually more precise and structured than casual everyday conversation. Academic English often requires clear arguments, evidence, logical organization and appropriate vocabulary.

VOCABULARY

Useful academic words

Learn words that frequently appear in academic writing and discussions.

VERB

Analyze

To examine something carefully in order to understand its structure, meaning or importance.

Example: The researchers analyzed the results of the experiment.

VERB

Assess

To evaluate or judge the quality, importance or effectiveness of something.

Example: The study assessed the effectiveness of the program.

VERB

Demonstrate

To show something clearly, often by providing evidence or an example.

Example: The results demonstrate a significant improvement.

VERB

Establish

To prove, discover or establish something with evidence.

Example: The researchers established a relationship between the two variables.

VERB

Indicate

To show that something is likely to be true or to point toward a particular conclusion.

Example: The findings indicate that further research is needed.

VERB

Investigate

To examine a subject carefully in order to discover facts or information.

Example: The researchers investigated the causes of the problem.

VERB

Significant

Important or large enough to have an important effect.

Example: The study found a significant difference between the two groups.

NOUN

Evidence

Information or facts that support a conclusion or show that something is true.

Example: There is considerable evidence supporting the theory.

CONNECTING IDEAS

Academic linking words

Linking words help readers understand the relationship between different ideas.

Furthermore

Used to introduce an additional point.

Example: Furthermore, the results support the original hypothesis.

Consequently

Used to introduce a result or consequence.

Example: Costs increased; consequently, the project was delayed.

However

Used to introduce a contrasting idea.

Example: The method is inexpensive. However, it is not always reliable.

In contrast

Used to emphasize a difference between two ideas.

Example: In contrast, the second group showed little improvement.

Therefore

Used to introduce a logical conclusion.

Example: The sample was too small; therefore, the results should be interpreted cautiously.

In addition

Used to add another related idea.

Example: In addition, participants completed a questionnaire.

ACADEMIC WRITING

A simple essay structure

01

Introduction

Introduce the topic and clearly state the main argument or purpose of the essay.

02

Body Paragraphs

Present your main ideas, supporting evidence, explanations and examples.

03

Conclusion

Summarize the main points and reinforce the overall argument without introducing major new ideas.

WRITING TIPS

Write more effectively

Be precise

Choose words that communicate exactly what you mean instead of using vague language.

Support your claims

Use evidence, examples, data or references when making important academic claims.

Avoid unnecessary repetition

Use appropriate alternatives when repeating the same idea or word unnecessarily.

Keep your argument logical

Make sure each paragraph contributes to your overall argument and follows logically from the previous one.
